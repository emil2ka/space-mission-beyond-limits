# Game Overview

**Title:** SPACE MISSION: BEYOND LIMITS
**Genre:** cooperative engineering mission simulator, educational.
**Players:** exactly 4, each with their own role and their own panel. Desktop-first web + phone adaptation.
**One line:** *Build one spacecraft for four — and find out what each of your decisions led to.*
**Core feeling:** "WE built it, and it was OUR decision that saved it (or doomed it)".

## What makes it a game
1. **One shared spacecraft.** Any action by one player immediately changes the numbers and the 3D model for everyone.
2. **Trade-offs, not right answers.** A second RTG gives power but adds mass, cost and heat. See [Consequences](Consequences.md).
3. **Information asymmetry.** Each role sees its own part of the picture, especially during a blackout. See [Crises and Blackout](Crises%20and%20Blackout.md).
4. **Risk is the team's right.** The game warns but does not fix mistakes by itself. The commander can approve a risky setup — this is recorded.
5. **The report explains the causes.** "You installed a power-hungry instrument but did not add generation — the batteries drained during data collection." See [Mission Report](Mission%20Report.md).

## Why 3D
Decision [0002 Full 3D with animations](../06%20Decisions/0002%20Full%203D%20with%20animations.md). The spacecraft is assembled before your eyes: modules fly to their slots, rotors spin, the antenna turns toward Earth, the arm grabs a sample, the capsule burns up on atmospheric entry. 3D is feedback for the mechanics, not decoration: an overheated unit glows on the model, an overloaded power line turns red.
